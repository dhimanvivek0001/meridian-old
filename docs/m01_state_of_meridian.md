# State of Meridian: M1 executive one-pager

Data: H&M transactions, 20 Sep 2018 to 22 Sep 2020. Revenue in price-index units (H&M scaled prices).

## Headline
Revenue is in structural decline that began BEFORE COVID. The underlying trend fell 12.9% peak to low,
and revenue was down year-on-year in 10 of the last 11 full months.

## Three findings
1. **Fewer customers is the main driver.** Of the YoY revenue decline (Oct 2019 - Aug 2020),
   47% came from fewer active customers, 26% from lower purchase frequency, 26% from smaller baskets.
   Oct 2019 - Feb 2020 was already down 40 log points before any lockdown.

2. **Customers buy fewer items, but pricier ones.** Items per order fell YoY in 9 of 11 months
   (down to -16% in July 2020) while price per item rose in 7 of 11 (up to +9%).
   June 2020's -14% basket: two-thirds fewer items, one-third cheaper items.

3. **Online absorbed lockdown; demand is event-driven.** In April 2020 stores sold nothing; online orders
   rose 57% and grew 12% larger. Black Friday lifts a normal week by 88%. Season peaks late May,
   bottoms Christmas week. Unexplained recurring spike in late September (both years, +43% to +63%).

## What this means for the next modules
- Customer count is the biggest lever -> M2 segments + CLV, M3 churn: who is leaving and is it worth saving them?
- Shrinking items per order -> M8 recommender: can cross-sell restore 1 item per order?
- Event-driven peaks -> M7 forecasting: stock for Black Friday, summer sale, late-Sep event.
- Price per item rising -> M6 pricing: real price increases or category mix shift?

## Caveats
- Month-1 retention of genuinely new customers is about 12-15%. Higher figures for early 2019
  cohorts are inflated by left-censoring (data starts Sep 2018).
- Channel 1 = store / 2 = online is inferred, supported by the April 2020 store shutdown.
- Revenue is a price index, not euros. Euro impact sized from M2 onward with stated assumptions.
